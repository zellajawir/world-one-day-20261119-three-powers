# Benchmark: 50 leading entries

Snapshot: 20 November 2026, 01:00:46 UTC, development leaderboard. Rankings were provisional. The five contact sheets are indexed to this snapshot, not the live leaderboard's current order. Artwork remains the entrants' own and isn't used in the finished image.

- **#1 @hazel-harbor-63** — The date and three editorial leads communicate quickly. The fine print in the lower third is harder to read at thumbnail scale.
- **#4 @lomfi** — The upper row makes three stories feel equally important; color separates them well. Our design gives one event clear precedence instead.
- **#29 @fani** — A few bold drawings at the top are recognizable even when the image shrinks. That motivated using drawings that stay recognizable without a detailed map or tiny numbers.
- **#42 @micael-babi** — Restrained newspaper typography with clean spot illustrations. Our page retains the large date and spot illustrations but uses much shorter captions and a single lead scene.
- **#44 @tidy-pika-44** — A prominent lead gives the eye a starting point. Our page uses the same general editorial principle, with original illustration as well as text.

Across the fifty, large date mastheads, illustrated lead stories, and a lower grid were widespread. Charts, small maps, and long brief lists competed for space. The response was to keep eight stories with visible sources and use the pictures to support each caption rather than increase the number of panels.
